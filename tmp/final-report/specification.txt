Assignment Description 
Overview 
This assignment is designed to help you apply software engineering principles, including 
SDLC stages, Agile practices, UML modeling, and design patterns, in a practical team -
based project. You will work in pre -assigned groups to design and develop a real -world 
system using Java. Over the course of the semester, your team will go through multiple 
development phases, create a functional prototype, apply Agile (Scrum), demonstrate 
your progress, and submit a final working system along with the required documentation. 
 
- Groups: Already formed (6 members each) 
- Platform: Java-based web application 
- Assignment Weight: 20% of final module grade 
Note: In this assignment, students will be assessed both individually and as part of a team 
across all evaluation components.

Final Report Structure 
 
Section Details 
1. Cover Page (1 page) Include the following details on the first page of your 
report: 
• Project Title 
• Group Members’ Names and Student IDs 
• Module Name and Code (e.g., SE2030 –

• Submission Date 
2. Introduction (~1-2  
pages) 
Provide a clear overview of the project. 
Include the following: 
• Project Overview: Briefly describe what your 
project is about. 
• Objectives: State the main goals you aim to 
achieve through the system. 
• Target Users or Stakeholders: Identify who will 
use the system or who is affected by it. 
• Scope and Limitations: Clearly define what 
the system will include and what is out of 
scope. 
3. Requirements (~2-3 
pages) 
Present the gathered requirements for the system in a 
structured format. 
Include: 
• Functional Requirements: What the system 
must do (e.g., user login, search functionality). 
• Non-functional Requirements: Quality 
aspects such as performance, usability, and 
security.

Include: 
• Use Case Diagram: A visual representation of 
user interactions with the system. 
• Database Design / ER Diagram: Show how

• UI Sketches/Screenshots and Descriptions: 
Provide screenshots, wireframes, or written

showing working parts of your system.

(~1–2 pages)

• Agile Approach and Sprint Summary: Show

• Development Phases or Timeline: Provide a 
summary of key milestones and their timelines. 
7. Conclusion & Future 
Work (~1- 2 pages) 
Summarize the project outcome and reflect on future 
improvements. 
Include: 
• Summary of Achievements: What was 
completed successfully. 
• Challenges Faced: Key difficulties or blockers 
during the project.

- Late submissions will incur penalties. 
- All students must contribute equally. 
- Ethical risks must be identified early (e.g., data privacy, AI misuse). 
- You may apply the knowledge gained from the OOP module, along with your own self-
study, to develop the web-based application. 
 
Special Note: Use of AI Tools 
• AI tools may be used as a learning and support resource throughout this 
project. Students may use AI tools for requirements gathering, brainstorming 
ideas, exploring solution alternatives, understanding technologies, 
improving documentation, generating pro totypes, debugging, and learning 
programming concepts. 
• However, AI tools must not replace the student's own understanding and 
contribution. All students are expected to fully understand, explain, and 
defend their individual work, including requirements, design decisions, 
implementation, testing, and documentat ion, during project evaluations, 
presentations, and viva examinations. 
• Students must ensure that all submitted work is reviewed, validated, and 
appropriately modified based on their own understanding. Any work that 
cannot be adequately explained by the student will be treated as 
unauthorised assistance. Plagiarism, academic m isconduct, or excessive 
reliance on AI -generated content may result in zero marks and disciplinary 
action in accordance with university regulations.
